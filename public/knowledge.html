01 / EVERYDAY WELLBEING

Stress, worry and anxiety

Understand common stress responses and identify small ways to create a little space when worries feel intense.

Explore this topic
What you may notice

Racing thoughts, tension, restlessness, avoidance or difficulty concentrating can occur with stress and anxiety. These experiences have many possible causes.

A small practice

Pause and name what is happening: “I notice I am feeling worried.” Then choose one manageable next action, such as getting water, stepping outside or contacting someone you trust.

When to seek support

Consider speaking with a qualified health professional when worry persists, feels difficult to manage or affects sleep, work, school or relationships.

02 / EVERYDAY WELLBEING

Low mood and emotional health

Learn how changes in mood, interest, energy and everyday routines can affect wellbeing.

Explore this topic
What you may notice

Persistent sadness, loss of interest, fatigue, changes in sleep or appetite and difficulty managing usual activities may deserve attention. A list of symptoms cannot diagnose you.

A small practice

Make one task smaller than usual. Try a short walk, a simple meal or one message to a trusted person. Small actions are not a replacement for treatment.

When to seek support

Seek a professional assessment if these changes persist, worsen or interfere with daily life. Seek urgent help if you may harm yourself or are in immediate danger.

03 / EVERYDAY WELLBEING

Grief, loss and change

Explore why grief can look different from person to person and why there is no single timetable.

Explore this topic
What you may notice

Grief may affect emotions, concentration, sleep, relationships and the body. Feelings can change over time and may return around reminders or anniversaries.

A small practice

Choose a gentle routine, let a trusted person know what would help, and give yourself permission to decline activities when you need rest.

When to seek support

Consider additional support if grief feels overwhelming, you are isolated or daily functioning becomes difficult.

04 / RELATIONSHIPS

Communication and boundaries

Practical ways to express needs, listen with respect and recognise personal boundaries.

Explore this topic
A useful conversation frame

Try: “When this happens, I feel… What would help me is…” Choose a calm moment when it is safe to talk, and listen for the other person's needs too.

Remember

Boundaries describe what you can accept and what you will do to care for your safety and wellbeing. You are allowed to ask for space.

Safety first

Where there is intimidation, coercion or violence, prioritise individual safety and specialist support. Joint counselling or confrontation may not be safe in abusive situations.

05 / COMMUNITY SUPPORT

HIV, dignity and emotional wellbeing

Stigma-free information about emotional support alongside appropriate HIV health services.

Explore this topic
Dignity matters

People living with HIV deserve respectful care and privacy. HIV status is sensitive personal information; nobody should be pressured to disclose it in a public forum or on this prototype.

A small practice

When supporting someone, ask what kind of help they want rather than making assumptions. Encourage them to speak with their HIV care team about treatment questions.

Service boundary

Anon does not provide HIV testing, medical treatment or treatment-adherence case management. Use verified health services for clinical advice.

06 / LIFE STAGES

Children, young people and families

Supportive ways to listen, build life skills and make conversations about emotions less difficult.

Explore this topic
Start with listening

Use open questions, give time to answer and take young people's concerns seriously. Avoid shame, threats or dismissing feelings as “just a phase”.

A practical family activity

Invite each person to name one thing that felt difficult and one thing that helped this week. Participation should be optional and age appropriate.

Safeguarding

Children and young people need age-appropriate safeguards, trusted adults and verified referral routes. This prototype is not monitored and cannot handle safeguarding disclosures.

07 / COMMUNITY SUPPORT

Substance use and gambling harm

Understand how habits can affect relationships, finances and wellbeing without blaming the person.

Explore this topic
Look at impact, not labels

Notice whether a behaviour is causing distress, financial strain, conflict or difficulty meeting responsibilities. Shame can make it harder to ask for help.

A small practice

Write down one change you would like to make and one trusted person or qualified service you could approach. Do not stop prescribed medication without medical advice.

When to seek support

Consider qualified help when you feel unable to reduce harm on your own or when health, safety or finances are affected.

08 / LIFE STAGES

Pregnancy and after birth

Recognise that emotional wellbeing deserves attention during pregnancy and the months after childbirth.

Explore this topic
Make room for support

Emotional changes can occur during pregnancy and after birth. Persistent sadness, severe anxiety, frightening thoughts or difficulty coping should be discussed with a qualified health professional.

A practical support request

Tell someone you trust one specific thing that would help today, such as a meal, rest time or assistance with an appointment.

Urgent concerns

Confusion, hallucinations, severe agitation or risk of harm to the parent or baby require urgent professional assessment.

A GENTLE 7-DAY LEARNING JOURNEY

One small step.
At your own pace.

This is an optional learning prompt—not a treatment plan, challenge or test. You can skip days, repeat activities or stop whenever you wish.

DAY 01

Notice

Pause and name one feeling without judging it.

DAY 02

Connect

Think of one person who helps you feel respected and safe.

DAY 03

Rest

Choose one small routine that supports rest or recovery.

DAY 04

Move gently

Try a comfortable movement or a little time outdoors.

DAY 05

Set a boundary

Identify one reasonable limit or need you want to communicate.

DAY 06

Learn

Read one guide and write down one question for a professional.

DAY 07

Choose next

Decide what you would like to continue, change or ask for help with.

YOUR SAFETY COMES FIRST

Information is helpful.
It is not emergency care.

Anon is an educational prototype, not a monitored support line or clinical service. If you or someone else may be in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department now. Do not wait for this website.
