READ THE NUMBERS RESPONSIBLY

What these figures do—and don't—mean

  • The 5.8% figure is a population survey finding reported for 2024; it is not a diagnosis for any visitor.
  • The 2019 Statistics Botswana figure counts outpatient consultation events, including repeat consultations—not unique patients or current demand.
  • The workforce density is reported in the WHO Mental Health Atlas 2024 country profile; it should be quoted with its source and profile year.
  • Differences in source, year, definitions and collection methods mean these figures should not be compared as if they were one continuous dataset.

WHY COMMUNITY EDUCATION MATTERS

Evidence becomes useful
when communities can act.

The WHO 2024–2025 report recommends using STEPS findings to strengthen population-wide health promotion and tailor prevention to community needs. Anon can support that work through accessible, non-clinical education and referral information.

01 / EDUCATION

Make knowledge accessible

Plain-language information, awareness content, myth-versus-fact explainers and practical wellbeing activities.

02 / EARLY SUPPORT

Help people navigate

Explain when to seek professional help and provide a verified referral directory once GABS confirms partners.

03 / DIGNITY

Reduce stigma

Inclusive language, privacy-first pathways and respectful information on HIV-related wellbeing and psychosocial support.

04 / LEARNING

Measure responsibly

Use aggregate education and outreach indicators to improve programme planning without collecting sensitive personal stories.
