How Anon can support GABS's published programme areas

GABS AREA 01

HIV/AIDS care and support

Provide stigma-free wellbeing education, explain privacy and support pathways, and direct clinical questions to verified health services. Anon does not provide HIV testing or treatment.

Potential contribution: education and navigation
GABS AREA 02

Child and youth support

Develop age-appropriate life-skills information, safe help-seeking guidance and caregiver education subject to child-safeguarding review.

Potential contribution: learning resources
GABS AREA 03

Wellness education

Offer plain-language guides, myth-versus-fact content, practical wellbeing activities and shareable campaign resources for community education.

Potential contribution: prevention and awareness
GABS AREA 04

Psychosocial services

Explain support options and prepare a verified referral directory. Any real counselling or case support requires approved practitioners, operating protocols and consent.

Potential contribution: signposting, pending partner validation
GABS AREA 05

Vulnerable communities and dignity

Use respectful language, accessible design, low-data pages, user choice and content that supports self-reliance without blaming or labelling people.

Potential contribution: inclusive digital access

Privacy, dignity and programme accountability

01 / DATA MINIMISATION

Collect less

Do not put names, phone numbers, HIV status, diagnoses, counselling notes or personal stories into the public impact dashboard.

02 / AGGREGATION

Report safely

Publish totals only after re-identification risk review. Suppress small group counts and avoid breakdowns that expose individuals.

03 / GOVERNANCE

Assign ownership

GABS should approve metric definitions, role-based access, review frequency, retention, incident handling and publication sign-off.

04 / SAFEGUARDING

Protect people

Use age-appropriate content and clinically reviewed urgent-support guidance. Anon is not monitored and cannot receive crisis disclosures.

A SIMPLE RESULTS CHAIN

From activities
to meaningful change.

A measurement framework should show how activities are expected to contribute to outcomes, while staying honest about what the platform can and cannot prove.

ACTIVITIES

What GABS does

Education, awareness, psychosocial support, outreach and referrals through approved programmes.

OUTPUTS

What gets delivered

Sessions held, resources produced, learning completed and referrals appropriately offered.

SHORT-TERM OUTCOMES

What may improve

Knowledge, confidence to seek help, awareness of services and understanding of rights and support options.

LONG-TERM CONTRIBUTION

What GABS works towards

Dignity, hope, self-reliance and sustainable community wellbeing—measured through broader programme evaluation, not app analytics alone.

SOURCE AND GOVERNANCE NOTE

Public programme alignment
is a starting point.

GABS's public profile describes its mission as uplifting vulnerable communities through HIV/AIDS care, child and youth support, wellness education, psychosocial services and community-based programmes. The public project description also outlines health education, mental-health awareness, screening/referral and wellness activities. This is a secondary public programme description, not a substitute for an approved GABS strategy or board endorsement.
